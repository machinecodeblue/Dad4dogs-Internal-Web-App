# Decision

**Status:** accepted (2026-08-24) — partial
**Live spec:** `platform.md` (Client list — owner-first) and `customers.md` (Client list screen).
**What we took:** `/clients/` is **owner-first**. One card per customer (name, phone, **View** → customer **summary**). Dogs nest under that owner. Dog name → dog detail. **Book** on the dog row → visit create for that dog (so two dogs can be booked on different days).
**What we left:** “View” does **not** open the edit form — it opens the customer summary. No click-the-whole-header mystery target; **View** and **Book** are explicit. No extra same-day multi-dog booking wizard — Book per dog is enough.
**Why:** The page is named Clients. Dog-first run-on rows made it unclear who you were looking at and what a tap would do.
**Do not edit this file to change product rules.** Change `platform.md` / `customers.md` instead.

---

You have identified a critical UX flaw that often plagues CRM systems in the pet care industry. Because the page is labeled **Clients**, the user's mental model expects the human to be the primary entity. The human makes the booking, pays the invoice, and serves as the primary contact.

Currently, your list format reads: `[Dog Name] · [Owner Name] [Tag] [Phone]`. This creates a flat, "run-on sentence" structure where the dog is the visual anchor, and there are no clear interactive affordances (buttons) to tell the user what clicking that row will actually do.

Here is how you can restructure this list to align with user expectations and provide clear, distinct actions.

### 1. Reversing the Hierarchy

The visual weight must shift to the customer. The customer is the "Parent" record, and the dogs are the "Child" records. By grouping dogs under their owner, you also solve the problem of multi-dog households; Jamie Lundquist wouldn't need two separate list items if she owned two dogs; both would nest cleanly under her name.

### 2. Creating Clear Affordances

Instead of the entire row acting as a mysterious, clickable link, you need explicit buttons for your two primary use cases: viewing the profile and booking an appointment.

### Proposed UI Layout

Here is a markdown representation of how a client card should look on this page:

**Alexa Green**
*(905) 531-2805*
`[ 👤 View Client Profile ]`

**Dogs:**

* **Winston** `[ 🟡 VAX ]` `[ + Book ]`

---

**David Lundquist**
*(226) 688-5370*
`[ 👤 View Client Profile ]`

**Dogs:**

* **Lulu** `[ 🟡 VAX ]` `[ + Book ]`
* **Havana** `[ 🟡 VAX ]` `[ + Book ]`

### 3. User Interaction Flow

* **Viewing the Profile:** Clicking anywhere on the top half of the card, or explicitly clicking the `[ 👤 View Client Profile ]` button, drives the user to the detailed edit page we discussed previously.
* **Booking an Appointment:** By placing the `[ + Book ]` button directly on the dog's row, you immediately answer the system's next logical question: *Who is this booking for?* The user clicks "Book" next to Lulu, and the resulting booking form is already pre-filled with David as the owner and Lulu as the pet.

If a customer like yourself has multiple dogs, do you typically book them in for daycare on the exact same days, or do you need the UI to easily support booking Lulu for Tuesday and Havana for Thursday?
